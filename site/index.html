The problem

A waiting qubit loses its phase

In a real circuit most qubits spend a large share of the time waiting while others run two-qubit gates. Slow frequency drift and ZZ coupling to busy neighbours scramble the phase in that time.

Slow driftEach shot sees a slightly different qubit frequency, so the phases fan out and the average shrinks.
ZZ crosstalkA busy neighbour adds a phase that identical pulse trains leave in place. Staggered trains refocus it.
Pulses cost somethingEvery pulse has its own error, so we only place them in windows long enough to pay for them, and "no DD" is always a candidate.

Scope

What the data supports, and what we do not claim

Supported by our runs

  • Staggered DD recovered idle-qubit ⟨X⟩ best on ibm_fez and ibm_kingston.
  • The same ordering appeared in the crosstalk test on three devices.
  • Our auto-DD scores above Qiskit's DD pass in simulation on all six workloads, by a tiny margin on some (Mirror-5x4: +0.0003).
  • 33 passing tests, including that every DD train composes to the identity.

Not claimed

  • That the staggering gain is general. Over our own uniform DD it was +0.03 to +0.04 on hardware.
  • That fidelity of the busy qubits improves. CZ errors dominate them.
  • That simulated ZNE values above 1 are physical.
  • Anything across devices. Each device was measured in its own session (3 interleaved repeats for the spectator test, one run for the crosstalk test), and baselines drift between sessions.
